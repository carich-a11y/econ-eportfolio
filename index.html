Cover Letter

Academic Direction and Professional Development

I am a junior at Stanford pursuing a B.A. in Economics with minors in Art History and Political Science. Throughout the major, I have been especially drawn to economics courses with historical undertones and to courses that connect directly to everyday life. That combination has shaped how I think about the field: not simply as a set of models, but as a way to understand institutions, incentives, and markets in settings that feel tangible and lived.

I am pursuing a career in finance, and classes such as BUSGEN 110 (Corporate Finance) and ECON 140 (Financial Markets) have been central to that direction even though they did not culminate in final papers. They strengthened the technical and conceptual side of my economics education and helped me understand how markets, valuation, and capital structure operate in practice. More recently, I have been working on my capstone project in ECON 30 on the motherhood burden in the post-COVID workforce, which has broadened my perspective on labor, inequality, and the tools I use to present research, including website building and AI-assisted workflows through Cursor.

My summers have also played an important role in shaping my economic perspective. After my first year, I interned at Lexington Partners, a secondary private equity and co-investment firm in New York City, where I was introduced to the buy-side through information sessions, industry exposure, and discussions of continuation vehicles, Microsoft Office modeling workflows, and the firm's global footprint. After my second year, I interned at Blue Owl Capital on the Direct Lending team in the technology sector, where I supported live deal diligence, worked through data-room materials, evaluated credit strengths and risks, contributed to an investment committee memo on an aviation software company, and built a multi-case financial model forecasting growth, margins, and debt paydown capacity. This summer, I will be interning at J.P. Morgan in Investment Banking within the Financial Sponsors Group.

At Stanford, I have complemented these experiences with leadership in pre-professional organizations. I have served on the boards of Stanford Finance and Stanford Women in Business over the past two years, roles that have deepened my commitment to finance while also strengthening my leadership and collaboration skills. Together, these experiences have made finance feel not like a separate professional track from my academic work, but a continuation of the questions I have been asking in economics about markets, institutions, decision-making, and long-term strategy.

The papers in this portfolio reflect the broader range of settings in which I have applied those interests. My paper on Venice examines tourism demand, environmental risk, and the public justification for the MOSE barrier system, bringing together travel, place-based economics, and policy. My papers on the European Union explore debt, austerity, populism, and sovereignty, showing my interest in political economy and the institutional consequences of financial crisis. My work on automation in China considers how demographic change and labor scarcity accelerate capital substitution, while my writing on scandal, dealers, and commercialization in the art world reflects the impact of my Art History minor and my interest in how markets shape cultural value.

Looking forward, I hope to pursue a career in finance that eventually leads to a more specialized in-house role, whether through deeper work on the buy-side or through a legal and institutional specialization in the financial sector. This ePortfolio reflects that trajectory. It brings together my academic interests, professional development, and selected writing into a clear narrative about the kind of work I hope to continue doing: analytically rigorous, institutionally grounded, and attentive to the real industries and environments that economics helps explain.
