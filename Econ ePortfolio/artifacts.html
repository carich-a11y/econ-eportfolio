Tourism and Policy

Flooding and Tourism in Venice, Italy

Econ 155 | Co-authored research paper

This paper evaluates the economic justification for Venice's MOSE barrier by examining how repeated flooding affects tourism demand in a city where cultural significance and economic vulnerability are deeply intertwined.

I included this paper because it captures one of the clearest intersections between my interests in travel, public policy, and place-based economics. It shows the way I am drawn to settings where economics has immediate real-world consequences for cities, cultural heritage, and public investment.

European Political Economy

Greek Debt, Austerity, and the Eurozone

Economics of the EU | Analytical essay

This essay considers why many Greek citizens felt unjustly punished during the debt crisis, while also evaluating the fiscal and political logic behind austerity in the broader Eurozone framework.

I selected this piece because it reflects my interest in finance, sovereign risk, and the institutional consequences of crisis. It pushed me to think carefully about the gap between macroeconomic credibility and the lived burden of adjustment.

Institutions and Finance

Populism, Nationalism, and European Institutions

Economics of the EU | Analytical essay

This paper analyzes how the financial crisis and migration pressures contributed to populist and nationalist movements across Europe, with particular attention to sovereignty, institutional legitimacy, and economic insecurity.

I see this artifact as a continuation of my interest in how financial and economic disruption can reshape political behavior. It connects to the finance-oriented side of my studies by showing that market shocks and crisis management have consequences far beyond balance sheets alone.

Technology and Growth

Automation in China's Aging Population

ECON 131 | Group final paper

This paper argues that demographic change and labor scarcity will accelerate automation and robotics adoption in China, linking labor supply, industrial policy, and capital substitution.

I chose this project because it reflects another part of my economics training: thinking about long-run structural change and how firms and states respond to constraint. It also connects to my broader interest in finance by focusing on productivity, investment, and the strategic implications of economic transition.

Art and Market Systems

Art, Scandal, and Commercialization

Econ 238C | Essay collection

This writing explores scandal, dealers, and commercialization in the nineteenth-century art world, highlighting the role of publicity, mediation, and market institutions in the creation of artistic value and legacy.

This artifact reflects the influence of my Art History minor on the way I approach economics. It shows my interest in how markets operate within culture and how economic thinking can clarify systems of value far beyond traditional finance alone.
